# SOURCE_METHOD_REVIEW — primary published method vs our exact inference
**2026-11-03 JST | Literature/measurand review, not newly measured acoustic scoring.**
Primary paper: J. M. Arend, A. Neidhardt, C. Pörschmann, *Measurement and Perceptual Evaluation of a Spherical Near-Field HRTF Set*, 29th Tonmeistertagung (2016), https://audiogroup.web.th-koeln.de/PUBLIKATIONEN/Arend_TMT2016.pdf . Publisher ZIP/data: https://zenodo.org/records/4297951 . Details from paper section 2.1–2.3 pp. 357–360, section 3.2 pp. 360–361, discussion/license p. 361. Publisher paper itself reports CC BY-SA 4.0 for miro/SOFA near-field files; embedded original SOFA notice CC 3.0 BY-SA, so independent edition-specific rights review still required.
## Physics and measurement chain
1. Original measurements: Geithain RL906 speaker, anechoic chamber ~200 Hz low-frequency cut-off; head = Neumann KU100, moved/rotated on VariSphear system. Four full-sphere Lebedev radii .5/.75/1/1.5 m and 2702 directions each; five circular radii .25–1.5 m are a **different** data grid. Distance from loudspeaker to ear-canal entrance was checked by laser; this **does not prove** the exact SOFA numerical receiver-microphone plane. At the physical center of the dummy head a reference omnidirectional microphone was used for loudspeaker compensation **per source distance**. Distances stated in the source do not automatically denote source-to-head-center vs source-to-ear-ear canal reference in the SOFA coordinates.
2. Excitation was emphasized sine sweep. Original HRIRs were truncated/windowed, transformed to miro, modified with adaptive low-frequency extension (ALFE) at 200Hz and reference-based inverse magnitude/phase FIR filtering. The phase/group delay was manipulated and the final IR truncated to **128 taps at 48k**. Thus `Data.IR` sample zero and a shared `Data.Delay [1,2]` cannot justify absolute physical time-of-flight. **Relative** ITD may be measurable but requires original L/R and filter processing audit. Original paper's ITD study used onset-based **10x oversampling**; NF5 D0 proposes cross-correlation-based 8x interpolation: different estimands, not directly substitutable.
3. Final data were leveled/peak normalized, with restoration of radial loudness difference available from publisher factors. Do not multiply radial coefficients or claim absolute Pa without resolving version/time/gain reference and differences from rounded published tables.
4. The paper warns that close sound-source (<1m) point-source assumption may be violated, and speaker-to-head reflections may introduce additional ripple.
## Severe apparatus confounder (source's own technical evaluation)
The full-sphere Lebedev rig used a robot arm that shadowed/reverberated **rear** incident sound. Comparing Lebedev to circular-grid HRTFs, the paper reports for 0.5m FRONT mean response ripple ~0.55dB (SD .59) over 700Hz–20kHz; the 0.5m REAR spectral difference averaged **6.51dB (SD 4.61dB)**, with high-frequency attenuation ~10–15dB above 10kHz. These published figures concern source-specific comparison and broad bands, **NOT an independently established ILD bias within every registered 500–8000Hz band**. Do not project 6.51dB into our ILD gate.
**Interpretation**: a predictor can accurately learn the measurement apparatus shadow rather than head/pinna physics. Accordingly, G3b if eventually authorized must report a prospective **diagnostic** by front/rear spatial sectors, including apparatus sensitivity, while keeping original all-domain primary gates and no posthoc exclusion of difficult/rear directions. Claim maximum: reproduction of this particular postprocessed measured signal under this rig, not generic head scattering.
The original authors explicitly advise caution in using this Lebedev set for sensitive listening experiments and describe auralization as its strength. G4 subjective ASMR/distance presence cannot be inferred from any spatial prediction score.
## Perception boundary
In the original paper's exploratory headphone study, loudness-normalized distance judgments were not reliable on average; non-normalized levels gave strong perceived-distance effects. That study used *circular-grid* HRTFs and human listeners, not the 2702-node full sphere and not a direct NF5 ASMR listener test. Do not transfer subjective conclusions to NF5.
